What hull speed means

A displacement boat makes its own bow and stern waves as it moves. When the wave length matches the boat's waterline, the stern settles into the trough and the boat has to climb its own bow wave — a soft speed limit called hull speed: V = 1.34 × √LWL, with LWL in feet and V in knots. The speed-length ratio V / √LWL tells you where a boat sits: below ~1.34 is easy displacement cruising, and pushing much past it costs disproportionate power until a light hull can climb onto a plane. Longer waterlines are simply faster, which is why racing hulls are long and lean. Everything runs locally; nothing leaves your browser.
