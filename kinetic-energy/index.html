About kinetic energy & momentum

Kinetic energy is the energy of motion: KE = ½mv², measured in joules. Because it depends on velocity squared, doubling your speed quadruples the energy — which is why stopping distances and crash forces rise so steeply with speed. Momentum is p = mv, a vector that's conserved in collisions. The two connect neatly: KE = p² / (2m). A 1000 kg car at 20 m/s (72 km/h) carries 200 kJ of kinetic energy.
