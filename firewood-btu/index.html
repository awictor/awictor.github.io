Not all firewood is equal

A cord (128 cu ft stacked) of dense hardwood holds far more heat than the same volume of softwood, because heat comes from the wood's mass. Seasoned hickory and oak deliver around 24–27 million BTU per cord, while pine and cedar give roughly half that, so a cord of oak can be worth two cords of pine for heating. Softwoods still light easily and are great for kindling and shoulder-season fires. These figures assume wood seasoned to ~20% moisture — green or wet wood wastes much of its energy boiling off water and creates creosote. Buy by the full cord, stack it off the ground, and give it 6–12 months to dry. Everything runs locally; nothing leaves your browser.
