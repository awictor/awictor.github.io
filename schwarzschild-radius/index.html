Schwarzschild radius (event horizon)

About the event horizon

The Schwarzschild radius is the size an object must be compressed to for its escape velocity to reach the speed of light — the radius of a non-rotating black hole's event horizon: r = 2GM / c². It scales linearly with mass. The Sun would need to be squeezed to about 2.95 km, and the whole Earth to just 8.9 mm — smaller than a marble. Because r grows with M, supermassive black holes are enormous yet have a lower average density than water. Uses G = 6.674×10⁻¹¹ and c = 299,792,458 m/s.
