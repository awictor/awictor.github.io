Why the base rate matters

Bayes' theorem updates a probability with new evidence: P(H|E) = P(E|H)·P(H) / [P(E|H)·P(H) + P(E|¬H)·P(¬H)]. The famous surprise: with a rare disease (say 1% prevalence) and a 99%-accurate test, a positive result still only means about a 50% chance you're actually sick — because false positives from the huge healthy majority swamp the true positives. That's the base-rate fallacy, and it shows up in spam filters, security alerts, and screening programs alike. Everything runs locally; nothing leaves your browser.
