About thermal expansion

Most materials grow when heated. For a rod of length L₀ and linear expansion coefficient α, a temperature rise ΔT lengthens it by ΔL = α·L₀·ΔT. For an isotropic material the area coefficient is 2α and the volume coefficient is 3α, so ΔA = 2α·A₀·ΔT and ΔV = 3α·V₀·ΔT. Coefficients are in parts per million per kelvin (e.g. steel ≈ 12×10⁻⁶). A negative ΔT means cooling and contraction. This is why bridges have expansion joints and rails leave gaps. Everything runs locally; nothing leaves your browser.
