How to play the Game of Y

Y is a connection game on a triangular, hexagon-tiled board. Two players — Red and Blue — take turns placing one stone of their colour on any empty cell. Stones never move or get captured.

The goal

Build one connected chain of your colour that touches all three sides of the triangle at once. The three corners count toward both sides they touch. The first player to complete such a chain wins immediately.

Why there are no draws

Y is draw-proof: on any completely filled board, exactly one player has a winning chain — never zero, never both. Trillium proves this with the reduction: repeatedly collapse every little triangle of three cells into a single cell holding the majority colour. Each collapse shrinks the board by one row while preserving the winner, until a single crowning stone reveals who won. Press Reduction ▸ after a game to watch it.

The pie / swap rule

Going first is a real advantage. With the pie rule on, after Red's opening move the second player may Swap — taking over that strong opening stone instead of replying — which keeps the game fair.

The AI

The opponent uses Monte-Carlo Tree Search. Because Y has no draws, every random playout ends in a clean win or loss with no fuzzy scoring — so the search converges to genuinely strong, principled play. Turn on the thinking heatmap to watch which moves the engine is exploring, brighter where it searches hardest.

Bridges

Two stones one knight-ish step apart, sharing two empty cells between them, form a bridge: even if the opponent plays one of the two connecting cells, you take the other and stay connected. The bridge coach highlights your bridges and warns when one is threatened.
