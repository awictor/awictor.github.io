Four-Square Cipher

Digraph substitution with two keyword squares

How the Four-square cipher works

Four 5×5 grids sit in a 2×2 block. The top-left and bottom-right are the plain alphabet; the top-right and bottom-left are mixed with two keywords. To encrypt a pair of letters, find the first in the top-left grid and the second in the bottom-right grid, then read the ciphertext letters from the top-right (row of the first, column of the second) and bottom-left (row of the second, column of the first) grids. Because it enciphers digraphs, it resists single-letter frequency analysis better than simple substitution. I and J share a cell; odd-length text is padded with X. Everything runs locally.
