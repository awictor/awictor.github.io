About Tap Code

Tap Code arranges the alphabet in a 5×5 Polybius square (with K sharing C's cell) and sends each letter as two bursts of taps: first the row, then the column. So A is tap · pause · tap, and Z is five taps, pause, five taps. It needs nothing but a way to make noise, which is why American POWs in Vietnam used it to communicate through cell walls. It's slower than Morse but far easier to learn and to tap out. Here taps are shown as dots; decode by entering "row-col" pairs. Everything runs locally; nothing leaves your browser.
