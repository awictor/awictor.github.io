How it works

  • A neutral density filter blocks light evenly, letting you use a slower shutter in bright light — for silky water, cloud streaks, or blurring crowds.
  • Each stop the filter removes doubles the exposure time. An ND64 (6 stops) multiplies your time by 26 = 64×.
  • So a metered 1/125 s behind an ND1000 (10 stops) becomes about 8 seconds — a completely different look.
  • Meter without the filter (or with your camera's live view), then apply the multiplier. Very dark filters may need manual focus, since autofocus can struggle.
  • For exposures past ~30 s you'll usually switch to Bulb mode and a remote or timer.
