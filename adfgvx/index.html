How ADFGVX works

Used by the German army from 1918, ADFGVX first drops each of the 26 letters and 10 digits into a keyword-mixed 6×6 grid whose rows and columns are labelled A D F G V X (letters chosen to be distinct in Morse code). Every character becomes a pair of those labels — a fractionation step. That doubled string is then written under a second transposition keyword and its columns are read off in alphabetical order, scrambling the pairs apart. The two stages together — substitution then transposition — made it one of the hardest field ciphers of its era. Everything runs locally.
