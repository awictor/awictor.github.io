About exposure value

Exposure value (EV) is a single number for a combination of aperture and shutter speed: EV = log₂(N² / t), where N is the f-number and t the shutter time in seconds. Each +1 EV is one stop = half the light. Raising ISO lowers the EV needed, by log₂(ISO/100). The Sunny 16 rule says a sunny scene at f/16 with shutter ≈ 1/ISO gives about EV 15 at ISO 100. Any aperture/shutter pair with the same EV is an "equivalent exposure" — that's how you trade depth of field against motion blur. Everything runs locally; nothing leaves your browser.
