About the Child-Pugh score

The Child-Pugh score (originally Child-Turcotte-Pugh) grades the severity of chronic liver disease from five factors — bilirubin, albumin, INR, ascites, and encephalopathy — each scored 1–3 points for a total of 5–15. That total maps to a class: A (5–6, well-compensated), B (7–9, significant compromise), or C (10–15, decompensated). It's widely used to estimate prognosis and surgical risk and to guide transplant discussion. This is an educational aid, not medical advice. Everything runs locally; nothing leaves your browser.
