Why wet-bulb matters

The wet-bulb temperature is the lowest temperature you can reach by evaporating water into the air — it's what a wet thermometer reads in a breeze. It matters for heat safety because sweat can only cool you down to the wet-bulb temperature. Sustained exposure near 35 °C wet-bulb is considered the limit of human survivability, even for healthy people at rest. This uses Stull's formula, accurate to a few tenths of a degree for typical conditions. Everything runs locally; nothing leaves your browser.
