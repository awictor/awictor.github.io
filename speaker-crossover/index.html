How it works

  • A first-order crossover uses one part per driver: a capacitor in series with the tweeter (blocks bass) and an inductor in series with the woofer (blocks treble).
  • At the crossover frequency each part's reactance equals the driver impedance: C = 1/(2πfR) and L = R/(2πfR) → R/(2πf).
  • A 2.5 kHz crossover into 8 Ω wants about 8 µF and 0.5 mH.
  • First-order rolls off gently at 6 dB/octave with minimal phase shift — simple and cheap, but little driver protection near the crossover.
  • Use the driver's impedance at the crossover frequency (often above nominal); a Zobel network can flatten a rising impedance first.
