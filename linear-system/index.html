About

A system of linear equations — like 2x + y = 5, x + 3y = 10 — is written in matrix form Ax = b and solved by Gaussian elimination: rows are combined to reach an upper-triangular form, then back-substitution recovers each unknown. Partial pivoting (swapping in the largest available pivot) keeps it numerically stable. If the coefficient matrix is singular (determinant 0), there is no unique solution — the equations are dependent or contradictory. Everything runs locally; nothing leaves your browser.
