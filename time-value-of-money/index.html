About the time value of money

A dollar today is worth more than a dollar tomorrow, because it can earn interest. The single-sum relationship is FV = PV·(1 + r)ⁿ, where r is the rate per period and n the number of periods. Rearranged, you can find the present value of a future amount (PV = FV/(1+r)ⁿ), the implied rate that grows PV into FV (r = (FV/PV)^(1/n) − 1), or how many periods it takes (n = ln(FV/PV)/ln(1+r)). A quick sanity check: the Rule of 72 says money doubles in roughly 72/rate% periods. Everything runs locally; nothing leaves your browser.
