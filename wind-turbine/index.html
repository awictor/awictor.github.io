How wind turbine power works

A turbine's power rises with the cube of wind speed: P = ½ · ρ · A · v³ · Cp, where ρ is air density (~1.225 kg/m³ at sea level), A is the rotor's swept area (πr²), v is wind speed, and Cp is the power coefficient — the fraction of wind energy the rotor captures. Because doubling wind speed gives 8× the power, siting matters enormously. No rotor can exceed the Betz limit of 16/27 ≈ 0.593; good real turbines reach Cp ≈ 0.35–0.45.
