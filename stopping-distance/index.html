How it works

  • Thinking distance is how far you travel before the brakes bite — speed × reaction time. It grows linearly with speed.
  • Braking distance = v² ÷ (2 · μ · g). It grows with the square of speed, so doubling your speed roughly quadruples the braking part.
  • Grip (μ) is everything: wet halves it, ice cuts it to a tenth — the same speed needs far more room.
  • These are idealized flat-road figures with good brakes and tyres; real distances are longer with load, grade, and worn components.
  • Leave a gap: the 2-second rule roughly matches a 1.5 s reaction plus margin at road speeds.
