About Snell's law

Snell's law governs how light bends crossing between media: n₁·sinθ₁ = n₂·sinθ₂, where n is each medium's refractive index (air ≈ 1.00, water ≈ 1.33, glass ≈ 1.5, diamond ≈ 2.42). Going into a denser medium (higher n) bends the ray toward the normal; the reverse bends it away. When leaving a denser medium at a steep enough angle — past the critical angle θc = arcsin(n₂/n₁) — the ray can't escape and reflects entirely (total internal reflection), the principle behind fiber optics and a diamond's sparkle.
