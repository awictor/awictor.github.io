About the Sharpe ratio

The Sharpe ratio measures return earned per unit of risk: (Rp − Rf) / σ, where Rp is the portfolio return, Rf the risk-free rate, and σ the volatility (standard deviation of returns). Higher is better — a Sharpe above 1 is generally considered good, above 2 very good. The Sortino ratio is a variant that only penalizes downside volatility, since investors don't mind upside swings. To annualize a per-period Sharpe, multiply by √(periods per year) — e.g. √12 for monthly, √252 for daily. Everything runs locally; nothing leaves your browser.
