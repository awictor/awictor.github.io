# Osculant

A zoomable garden of Ford circles where every fraction kisses its Farey neighbors.

## Why it's cool

For every reduced fraction `p/q`, Osculant draws a circle tangent to the number
line at `p/q` with radius `1/(2q²)`. The payoff is that **two circles touch
exactly when their fractions are Farey neighbors** — i.e. when `|ps − qr| = 1` —
producing an infinitely nested lattice of kissing bubbles that is one of number
theory's most beautiful pictures. Pan and zoom along the line and finer
denominators bloom into the gaps by the mediant rule; hover any circle to see the
math behind why it sits where it does.

It's a genuine number-theory explorer: Farey sequences, mediants, continued
fractions, and the Stern–Brocot tree, made tangible and interactive.

## Features

- **Ford circles** for the visible window, generated by bounded Stern–Brocot
  enumeration up to a max-denominator budget (not brute force), so the on-screen
  circle count stays roughly constant at any zoom.
- **Smooth pan & zoom** with eased camera motion and a level-of-detail fade, so
  finer denominators bloom in/out instead of popping.
- **Auto-LOD** raises the denominator budget as you zoom in, so flying deep into
  the lattice keeps producing new circles.
- **Hover / tap to inspect**: fraction, decimal, continued-fraction expansion
  `[a0; a1, a2, …]`, the two tangent Farey parents, the literal
  `|p·s − q·r| = 1` computation, and Stern–Brocot depth. On hover the actual
  tangency (kissing) points to both neighbors are sparked.
- **Stern–Brocot minimap** drawing the L/R path to the focused fraction.
- **Irrational flight**: type a real (or use the π / e / √2 / φ presets) and the
  camera flies to it, landing its continued-fraction convergents circle by
  circle — with a nod to φ as the worst-approximable number.
- **Color modes**: hue by denominator, or hue by Stern–Brocot depth.
- **2× PNG export** (true re-render, not an upscaled bitmap) and a **shareable
  URL hash** encoding center, zoom, max denominator, color mode, and the pinned
  fraction.

## Run it

No build, no dependencies, no network calls. Open the file:

```
# just open it
index.html        # double-click, or drag into a browser

# or serve it statically
python -m http.server 8000   # then visit http://localhost:8000
```

Works in any modern browser, desktop or touch.

## Controls

**Desktop**
- Drag to pan.
- Wheel to zoom (about the cursor).
- Hover a circle to inspect it; click a circle to pin it.
- Click empty space above the axis to fly there.

**Touch**
- One-finger drag to pan.
- Two-finger pinch to zoom.
- Tap a circle to inspect/pin it; tap empty space above the axis to fly there.

**Panel**
- **Detail** slider — max denominator (density). Adjusting it turns off auto-LOD.
- **Color** — toggle hue by denominator vs. by Stern–Brocot depth.
- **Fly** — enter a real (`pi`, `e`, `sqrt2`, `phi`, or any number) to animate the
  convergents converging on it. Presets: π, e, √2, φ.
- **Export PNG** — save the current view at 2× resolution.
- **Copy link** — copy a URL that restores the exact view.
- **Reset** — return to the default `[0, 1]` view.

## The math, briefly

- **Ford circle**: the circle of radius `1/(2q²)` tangent to the real line at the
  reduced fraction `p/q`. Two Ford circles for `p/q` and `r/s` are tangent iff
  `|ps − qr| = 1`.
- **Mediant**: the mediant of `a/b` and `c/d` is `(a+c)/(b+d)`. In the
  Stern–Brocot tree, each fraction is the mediant of its two nearest ancestors —
  which are precisely its two tangent Ford neighbors.
- **Continued fraction**: the convergents of a real number are its best rational
  approximations; Osculant lands them one at a time when you fly to a real.
