The 4:1 ladder rule

A straight or extension ladder is safest at the 4:1 angle: for every 4 feet of height where the ladder touches the wall, set its base 1 foot out from the wall — base = contact height ÷ 4. That puts it near 75.5°, steep enough not to slide out but not so steep it tips back. The straight-line length to the contact point is the hypotenuse, √(height² + base²), but buy a longer ladder than that: it must extend about 3 ft above the roof edge or platform for a safe handhold, and you should never stand on the top three rungs. Tie off the top, level the feet, and keep your belt buckle between the rails. Everything runs locally; nothing leaves your browser.
