Why the carbon-to-nitrogen ratio matters

Compost microbes eat carbon for energy and nitrogen to build protein. They work fastest when the pile sits near a C:N ratio of about 25–35:1. Too much carbon (dry leaves, cardboard, straw) and the pile breaks down slowly; too much nitrogen (grass, food scraps, manure) and it turns wet and smells of ammonia. Browns are high-carbon materials with a large ratio; greens are high-nitrogen with a small ratio. Assuming similar carbon content, the combined ratio is the mass-weighted harmonic mean: Σmass ÷ Σ(mass ÷ ratio). The ideal green share is the fraction of total mass that should be greens to land on your target. Everything runs locally; nothing leaves your browser.
