The spread rule

Rising air cools at about 3°C per 1,000 ft while its dew point drops only ~0.5°C, so the two converge at roughly 2.5°C per 1,000 ft. Cumulus clouds form where they meet, so the base sits near (temperature − dew point) ÷ 2.5 × 1,000 feet above the surface. A small spread means low cloud (or fog when it reaches zero); a large, dry spread means high bases or clear skies. This is a fair-weather estimate for convective cumulus.
