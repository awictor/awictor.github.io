How it works

  • A capo raises every open string by the number of frets it clamps, so a familiar chord shape sounds in a higher key.
  • Each fret is one semitone. Play a G shape with the capo on 2 and it sounds in A; on 4, it's B.
  • To sing in a tricky key, find easy open shapes a few frets lower and capo up to match — the “capo fret” is just the semitone gap between the two keys.
  • The shape's chord quality stays the same (major stays major); only the pitch moves.
  • Notes wrap around the 12-note chromatic scale, so a capo on 12 sounds an octave up in the same key.
