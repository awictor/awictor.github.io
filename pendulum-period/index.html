About the simple pendulum

For small swings, a simple pendulum's period depends only on its length and gravity — not its mass or (much) its amplitude: T = 2π·√(L/g). So the period grows with the square root of length: quadruple the length to double the period. A 1-metre pendulum swings in about 2 seconds, and a "seconds pendulum" (each swing exactly 1 s, so T = 2 s) is about 0.994 m long — the basis of the pendulum clock. Change g to see how it would swing on the Moon. Everything runs locally; nothing leaves your browser.
