How it works

  • An interval is the distance between two pitches, measured in semitones (half steps) — the atoms of Western harmony.
  • Counting up from the first note: 7 semitones is a perfect 5th, 4 is a major 3rd, 3 a minor 3rd, and 6 the restless tritone.
  • Intervals here wrap within one octave, so A up to C is a minor 3rd (3 semitones), not a descending 6th.
  • Major/perfect intervals sound bright and stable; minor and the tritone sound darker or more tense.
  • Stack a major 3rd then a minor 3rd from a root and you've built a major triad — intervals are how chords and scales are spelled.
