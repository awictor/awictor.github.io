About bond pricing

A bond's price is the present value of its future cash flows: each coupon plus the face value at maturity, discounted at the yield. Price = Σ C/(1+y)ᵗ + F/(1+y)ⁿ, where C is the coupon per period and y the yield per period. When the yield equals the coupon rate the bond trades at par; a lower yield means a premium (price above face), a higher yield a discount. Yield to maturity is the single rate that makes the discounted cash flows equal the market price — solved here numerically. Current yield is just annual coupon ÷ price.
