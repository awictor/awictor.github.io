How wire ampacity works

Ampacity is the current a conductor can carry continuously without overheating. For copper wire (NEC 75 °C column) it runs about 14 AWG → 20 A, 12 → 25, 10 → 35, 8 → 50, 6 → 65, and up. Lower gauge numbers mean thicker wire and more capacity. Real installations get derated — bundling several current-carrying conductors, high ambient temperature, or continuous loads (multiply by 0.8) all lower the usable ampacity — and small branch circuits are also limited by the breaker (14 AWG → 15 A, 12 → 20 A, 10 → 30 A).
