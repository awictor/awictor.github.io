About complex numbers

A complex number a + bi pairs a real part with an imaginary one (i² = −1). Addition works component-wise; multiplication uses (a+bi)(c+di) = (ac−bd) + (ad+bc)i; division multiplies by the conjugate of the denominator. The modulus |z| = √(a²+b²) is its distance from the origin, and the argument is its angle. In polar form r·e^(iθ), multiplication just multiplies the moduli and adds the angles — which is why complex numbers are the natural language for rotations, AC circuits, and signal processing.
