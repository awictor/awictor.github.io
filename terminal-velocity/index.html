About terminal velocity

A falling object speeds up until air resistance balances gravity; from then on it falls at a constant terminal velocity. Setting drag equal to weight, ½ρv²Cd·A = mg, and solving gives v = √(2mg / (ρ·A·Cd)), where ρ is air density, A the cross-sectional area facing the airflow, and Cd the drag coefficient. Heavier or more streamlined objects fall faster; larger area or higher drag slows them. A belly-down skydiver reaches ~55 m/s; head-down, well over 90 m/s.
