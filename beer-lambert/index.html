About the Beer-Lambert law

In spectrophotometry, absorbance is proportional to how much light-absorbing substance the beam passes through: A = ε · c · l, where ε is the molar absorptivity (how strongly the species absorbs at that wavelength), c the concentration, and l the path length. Absorbance relates to transmittance by T = 10⁻ᴬ (so A = 1 means 10% of light gets through, A = 2 means 1%). Because A is linear in c, a spectrophotometer reading gives concentration directly once ε and l are known — the workhorse of chemistry and biology labs.
