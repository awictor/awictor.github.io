About matter waves

In 1924 Louis de Broglie proposed that every particle has a wavelength λ = h/p, where h is Planck's constant and p is momentum. For a slow particle p = mv, so λ = h/(mv); given kinetic energy instead, λ = h/√(2mE). Heavier or faster objects have vanishingly small wavelengths — which is why matter looks like particles at everyday scales — but electrons have wavelengths comparable to atomic spacings, the basis of electron microscopy and diffraction.
