Why things float

Archimedes' principle: a submerged object is pushed up by a force equal to the weight of the fluid it displaces, F = ρ·V·g. If that buoyant force exceeds the object's weight — equivalently, if the object's average density is less than the fluid's — it floats. Apparent weight is the object's real weight minus the buoyant force, which is why a rock feels lighter underwater. Uses g = 9.80665 m/s². Everything runs locally; nothing leaves your browser.
