What room modes are

Between two parallel walls, sound waves whose half-wavelength divides evenly into the distance reinforce themselves into standing waves — these are axial room modes. Their frequencies are f = n · c / (2L), where c is the speed of sound (~343 m/s at 20 °C, 331.3 + 0.606·T), L is the wall spacing, and n = 1, 2, 3… At those frequencies the room boosts or cancels bass depending on where you sit, which is why some spots sound boomy and others thin. Rooms where two dimensions share a mode (or are simple multiples) have the worst pile-ups; aim for spread-out modes and treat the lowest ones with bass traps in the corners.
