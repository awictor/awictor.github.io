About AWG

American Wire Gauge sizes conductors on a logarithmic scale — a smaller number means a thicker wire. The diameter is d = 0.127 mm × 92^((36 − n)/39), so each 6-gauge step roughly halves the cross-sectional area, and going from AWG 10 to AWG 20 cuts the diameter about threefold. Cross-sectional area (which sets current capacity and resistance) is π/4 · d². Gauges above 0 continue as 1/0 (enter 0), 2/0 (−1), 3/0 (−2), and so on.
