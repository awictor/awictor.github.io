Why three answers?

When the numbers are positive, all definitions of a mod n agree. But with negatives they diverge — the classic -7 mod 3 is -1 in C/JavaScript (truncated), 2 in Python (floored), and 2 in the Euclidean sense (always non-negative). Each keeps a different identity: truncated makes the quotient round toward zero, floored rounds the quotient down, and Euclidean guarantees 0 ≤ r < |n|. Knowing which your language uses prevents subtle bugs in hashing, array wrapping, and clock math. Everything runs locally; nothing leaves your browser.
