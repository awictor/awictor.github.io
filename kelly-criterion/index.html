About the Kelly Criterion

The Kelly Criterion gives the bet size that maximizes the long-run growth rate of your bankroll: f* = (b·p − q) / b, where p is the win probability, q = 1−p, and b is the net payout odds. Betting more than f* increases risk without improving growth; betting less is safer but slower. Because real-world probabilities are uncertain, many practitioners use fractional Kelly (half or quarter) to cut volatility. A negative result means you have no edge — don't bet.
