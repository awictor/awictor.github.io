About the A-a gradient

The alveolar-arterial oxygen gradient is the difference between the oxygen in the alveoli (calculated) and in the arterial blood (measured) — it tells you why someone is hypoxic. The alveolar oxygen comes from the alveolar gas equation: PAO₂ = FiO₂·(Patm − PH₂O) − PaCO₂/R (sea level: Patm 760, water vapour 47, respiratory quotient 0.8). A widened gradient points to a diffusion problem, shunt, or V/Q mismatch, while a normal gradient with hypoxia suggests hypoventilation or altitude. Normal rises with age (≈ age/4 + 4).
