How the 500 rule works

Point a camera at the night sky and the stars slowly streak as the Earth turns. The 500 rule gives the longest exposure before that trailing shows: seconds = 500 / (focal length × crop factor). Multiplying focal length by the sensor's crop factor gives the full-frame equivalent — a longer effective lens magnifies the motion, so it needs a shorter exposure. Modern high-resolution sensors show trailing sooner, so many shooters use the stricter 300 (or even 200) rule instead. The sky drifts about 15 arcseconds per second of time at the celestial equator, which is what sets the limit. Longer than this and you'll want a star tracker.
