About the BUN/creatinine ratio

Dividing blood urea nitrogen by serum creatinine (both in mg/dL) helps localize the cause of kidney dysfunction. A normal ratio is roughly 10–20:1. A high ratio (>20) points to a prerenal cause such as dehydration or GI bleeding, where the kidney reabsorbs more urea. A low ratio (<10) suggests intrinsic renal disease, low protein intake, or liver disease. It's read alongside the absolute values and clinical picture. Educational aid, not medical advice.
