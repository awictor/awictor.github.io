Shutter speed

The 180-degree rule

On a rotary shutter, the angle sets how long each frame is exposed: shutter speed = angle / (360 × fps). The classic 180° gives an exposure of half the frame time — at 24 fps that's 1/48 s — and produces natural-looking motion blur. Wider angles (270°, 360°) add blur and light; narrower angles (90°, 45°) give a crisp, staccato look. To keep the 180° look when you change frame rate, keep the angle fixed and let the shutter speed follow. Everything runs locally; nothing leaves your browser.
