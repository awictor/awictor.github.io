About photon energy

A photon's energy is set entirely by its frequency: E = hf = hc/λ, where h is Planck's constant (6.62607015×10⁻³⁴ J·s) and c is the speed of light (299,792,458 m/s). Shorter wavelengths (bluer light) carry more energy; longer ones (redder, infrared) carry less. Energies are tiny in joules, so physicists use electronvolts (1 eV = 1.602176634×10⁻¹⁹ J) — visible light spans roughly 1.6–3.3 eV. This underpins spectroscopy, solar-cell design, LED and laser engineering, and photochemistry. All constants are the 2019 SI exact values; everything runs locally.
