How cold brew ratios work

Cold brew is just ground coffee steeped in cold water for 12–24 hours, and its strength is set by the coffee-to-water ratio. A ratio is written as 1 : N, meaning N parts water per 1 part coffee, so coffee = water ÷ N. A tight 1:5–1:8 makes a concentrate you dilute to taste (a 1:1 pour with water or milk roughly doubles the volume); 1:15–1:17 brews a ready-to-drink batch. Weigh both coffee and water in grams for repeatability (1 ml water ≈ 1 g). Use a coarse grind, and remember the grounds soak up and hold back some liquid, so your yield is a little less than the water you started with. Everything runs locally; nothing leaves your browser.
