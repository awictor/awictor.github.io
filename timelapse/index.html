How timelapse timing works

A timelapse takes one frame every interval and plays them back at a normal frame rate, compressing hours into seconds. The number of frames is shoot duration ÷ interval; the clip length is frames ÷ fps. So an hour shot every 5 s is 720 frames, which at 24 fps plays back in 30 seconds — a 120× speed-up. To hit a target clip length, work backwards: shoot time = clip seconds × fps × interval. Shorter intervals give smoother, longer clips but fill your card faster; slower subjects (clouds, stars, construction) want longer intervals. Everything runs locally; nothing leaves your browser.
