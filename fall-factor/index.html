How it works

  • Fall factor = fall length ÷ rope paid out. It ranges 0–2 in normal climbing (2 is a fall directly onto the belay with no rope to absorb it).
  • The counterintuitive part: impact force depends on the factor, not the height. A short fall on little rope can hit harder than a long fall on lots of rope.
  • Peak force ≈ mg + √((mg)² + 2 mg R f), treating the rope as a spring of modulus R.
  • More rope out means more stretch to absorb the fall — softer catch, lower force. Dynamic ropes keep peak force under the UIAA 12 kN limit even at factor 2.
  • Idealized model for learning; real forces depend on knots, belay slippage, friction, and rope age.
