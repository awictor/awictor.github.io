What is a modular inverse?

The modular multiplicative inverse of a modulo m is the number x with a·x ≡ 1 (mod m) — the modular equivalent of dividing by a. It exists only when a and m are coprime (gcd = 1). The extended Euclidean algorithm finds it in one pass, also returning the Bézout coefficients where a·x + m·y = gcd(a, m). Modular inverses are essential in RSA key generation (the private exponent) and in solving modular equations. Everything runs locally; nothing leaves your browser.
