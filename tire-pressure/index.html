Why pressure drifts

Gas pressure tracks absolute temperature (Gay-Lussac's law), so a cold snap deflates your tires and a hot highway pumps them up — roughly 1 PSI for every 10°F. To do it right you work in absolute units: add 14.7 to gauge PSI and 459.67 to °F, scale by the temperature ratio, then convert back. That's why the door-jamb spec is a cold pressure — check it before driving, when the tires match the outside air. Everything runs locally; nothing leaves your browser.
