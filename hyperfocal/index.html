About hyperfocal distance

The hyperfocal distance H = f²/(N·c) + f is the focus distance that keeps everything from half that distance to infinity acceptably sharp — landscape photographers focus there to maximize depth of field. Here f is focal length, N the f-number, and c the circle of confusion (the largest blur spot still perceived as sharp, which scales with sensor size). Once you focus at some distance s, the near and far sharp limits are D_N = s(H−f)/(H+s−2f) and D_F = s(H−f)/(H−s); when you focus at or beyond H, the far limit is infinity.
