How it works

  • Flat beer already holds some dissolved CO₂ — the residual — set by the warmest temperature it saw during fermentation.
  • Priming sugar ferments in the bottle to make up the difference to your target. Dextrose yields about 4 g per liter per volume of CO₂.
  • Warmer fermentation leaves less residual CO₂, so you need more priming sugar.
  • Table sugar (sucrose) is a bit more efficient, so use roughly 91% of the dextrose weight.
  • Dissolve the sugar in a little boiled water, gently mix into the whole batch, then bottle — over-priming makes gushers or bottle bombs.
