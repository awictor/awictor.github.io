Two-Square Cipher

Digraph cipher — one step both ways

How the Two-square cipher works

Two keyword-mixed 5×5 squares are stacked vertically. For each pair of plaintext letters, find the first in the top square and the second in the bottom square, then read the ciphertext by swapping their columns — top square at (row of first, column of second), bottom square at (row of second, column of first). Because a column swap is its own inverse, the cipher is self-reciprocal: run the ciphertext through again with the same keywords to get the plaintext. It's a "double Playfair" that avoids Playfair's awkward double-letter rules. I/J share a cell; odd text is padded with X. Everything runs locally.
